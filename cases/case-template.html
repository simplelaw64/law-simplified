THE CASE IN ONE MINUTE

What happened?

This section gives someone who knows nothing about the case a quick explanation of what happened, what the dispute was about and why it reached court.

01

The Facts

Start with the story behind the case. Who were the parties? What happened between them? What caused the dispute?

Only include facts that actually help the reader understand the legal issue. A case study should not become a huge timeline of irrelevant information.

THE BIG QUESTION

What did the court actually have to decide?

Write the central legal issue here as clearly as possible.

For example: was the defendant legally responsible for the claimant's loss?

02

What did each side argue?

Court cases usually make more sense once you understand what each side was actually trying to convince the court to accept.

SIDE ONE

First party's argument

Explain the strongest part of this side's argument in straightforward language.

SIDE TWO

Second party's argument

Explain the competing argument and why the other side believed the court should decide differently.

03

What did the court decide?

Explain which side succeeded and what the court ultimately decided.

More importantly, explain the reasoning. What convinced the judge or judges? What legal rule did they apply?

IN SIMPLE TERMS

What does the judgment actually mean?

Translate the most important part of the decision into ordinary language without changing its legal meaning.

THE LEGAL PRINCIPLE

What rule came from the case?

Explain the important legal principle or development associated with the decision.

04

Why does this case matter?

This is where we move beyond simply telling the reader what happened.

Explain how the case affected the law, individuals, businesses, later cases or the way courts approach similar disputes.

THINKING ABOUT THE CASE

Was the decision convincing?

This section is where your own analysis can appear. Consider whether the reasoning was persuasive, whether the outcome seems fair, what competing interests existed and whether the law could have developed differently.

Your analysis should be clearly separated from what the court itself actually decided.

THE TAKEAWAY

If you remember one thing...

Give the reader one concise explanation of why this case is worth remembering.

SOURCES & FURTHER READING

Want to look deeper?

When we publish a real case study, this section will contain reliable sources such as the judgment, legislation and other authoritative legal material.
