Weller v Associated Newspapers Ltd

[2015] EWCA Civ 1176

The photographs were taken during an ordinary family outing in public. Could publication still invade privacy?

COURT

Court of Appeal

YEAR

2015

AREA OF LAW

Privacy Law

READ TIME

3 minutes

THE CASE IN ONE MINUTE

What happened?

Photographs were taken of Paul Weller's children during a family outing in California.

Mail Online later published seven photographs showing their faces.

The Court of Appeal upheld the decision that the children could have a reasonable expectation of privacy despite being in public.

01

The Facts

The children included a 16-year-old and two ten-month-old twins.

Weller objected to the photographer continuing to take pictures. The photographs were later published online.

THE BIG QUESTION

Can someone have a reasonable expectation of privacy even when photographed in public?

02

What did each side argue?

ASSOCIATED NEWSPAPERS

The activities happened in public

The photographs showed ordinary activities visible to people nearby.

THE CHILDREN

This was still private family time

They had not chosen publicity and argued that mass publication was different from simply being seen nearby.

YOUR TURN

What would you decide?

Should the children have had privacy protection even though the photographs were taken in public?

YOUR DECISION

READER RESULTS

RELEVANT LAW

Privacy and freedom of expression

Courts ask whether there was a reasonable expectation of privacy and, if so, balance Article 8 against Article 10.

03

What did the Court of Appeal decide?

The children's position was upheld.

The judges accepted that the photographs were taken in public, but that did not automatically remove privacy protection.

IN SIMPLE TERMS

Public place does not automatically equal no privacy.

The court looks at the whole situation.

THE LEGAL PRINCIPLE

Privacy can sometimes exist in public

A reasonable expectation of privacy can exist even in a public place, depending on the circumstances.

04

Why does this case matter?

The case shows the difference between being visible to people nearby and having an image deliberately published to a huge audience.

THINKING ABOUT THE CASE

Was the decision convincing?

I think it is convincing because public visibility and mass online publication are not necessarily the same thing.

THE TAKEAWAY

If you remember one thing...

Privacy does not necessarily disappear simply because someone is in public.
