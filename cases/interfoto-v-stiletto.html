CASE STUDY • CONTRACT LAW

Interfoto Picture Library Ltd v Stiletto Visual Programmes Ltd

[1987] EWCA Civ 6; [1989] QB 433

A company returned photographs late and faced a charge of almost £3,800. Could Interfoto enforce such a harsh term without clearly pointing it out?

COURT

Court of Appeal

YEAR

1987

AREA OF LAW

Contract Law

READ TIME

3 minutes

THE CASE IN ONE MINUTE

What happened?

Interfoto sent 47 photographic transparencies to Stiletto. A delivery note said that if they were kept for more than 14 days, Stiletto would have to pay £5 plus VAT per photograph, per day.

The photographs were returned late and Interfoto demanded almost £3,800.

The Court of Appeal decided that the condition was particularly onerous and unusual, so Interfoto needed to do more to bring it fairly to Stiletto's attention.

01

The Facts

Stiletto was an advertising company looking for photographs for a presentation. Interfoto sent 47 transparencies with a delivery note containing nine conditions.

One condition imposed a £5 plus VAT daily charge for every transparency kept beyond 14 days.

Stiletto returned them late. Interfoto invoiced £3,783.50, and the dispute became whether that condition had become part of the contract.

THE BIG QUESTION

If a contract contains an unusually harsh term, is printing it in the small print enough?

The court had to decide whether Interfoto had done enough to bring this condition to Stiletto's attention.

02

What did each side argue?

INTERFOTO

The terms were supplied

Interfoto argued that Stiletto received the conditions and had the opportunity to read them.

STILETTO

The charge needed a clearer warning

Stiletto argued that the term was too unusual and severe to be treated like ordinary small print.

YOUR TURN

What would you decide?

Should Interfoto be allowed to enforce the full late-return charge?

YOUR DECISION

BEFORE INTERFOTO

What did the law already say?

Contract law already required reasonable notice of written terms, and especially unusual terms could require stronger notice.

03

What did the court decide?

Stiletto succeeded on the key issue.

The Court of Appeal held that the late-return condition had not been incorporated because Interfoto had not sufficiently drawn attention to it.

IN SIMPLE TERMS

What does this mean?

If a term is especially harsh or surprising, the person relying on it may need to make it especially clear.

THE LEGAL PRINCIPLE

The bigger the surprise, the clearer the warning.

The more onerous or unusual a term is, the more notice may be required.

04

Why does this case matter?

Interfoto prevents businesses from assuming that every term is enforceable simply because it appears somewhere in the paperwork.

THINKING ABOUT THE CASE

Was the decision convincing?

I think it creates a sensible balance between responsibility for reading contracts and protection from unusually harsh surprises.

THE TAKEAWAY

If you remember one thing...

An unusually harsh contractual term may need to be brought clearly to the other party's attention before it can bind them.
